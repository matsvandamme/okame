In the box

The Okame egg (with the bird inside) · a USB-C cable to your MacBook · this booklet.

The egg plugs into the cable's USB end at its narrow side. Keep it on the desk next to your laptop; it is happiest where it can see you.

— 2 —
GETTING STARTED · はじめかた

Wake up your bird はじめかた

Plug it in

Put the cable on the egg's plug and the other end in your MacBook. The screen lights up: an egg, fast asleep.

Join its Wi-Fi

After a minute it shows JOIN OKAME-… and then a PASSWORD. On your iPhone, open Wi-Fi settings and join that network with that password.

Add your Wi-Fi

The Okame page opens by itself (if not, open 192.168.4.1 in Safari). Under Settings, pick your home Wi-Fi, type its password and tap Add network. Add your phone's hotspot too if you like.

It hatches!

Once it's on your Wi-Fi, the egg wobbles, cracks… and out comes your chick. Open the page again and give it a name. The page's address becomes name.local: add it to your Home Screen.

No Wi-Fi it knows? Then the bird simply sleeps (a little Wi-Fi sign blinks). Time still passes, but at most 12 hours count, so it can't come to harm while you're away.

— 3 —
YOUR REMOTE · リモコン

Your phone is the remote リモコン

The egg has no buttons: you look after your bird from your iPhone. Open name.local in Safari (use the name you gave it) while your phone is on the same Wi-Fi. Then tap Share → Add to Home Screen so it's always one tap away.

At the top you see the egg's screen live, with its food and joy hearts underneath. Soft chirps play on your phone (turn them off under Tune).

Care buttons

Seed: a full meal, fills a food heart. It refuses when it's full.
Millet: its favourite treat. Instant joy, but it makes it chubby.
Bath: splish splash, cleans up the droppings.
Vet: when a blinking cross appears, it feels poorly. The vet fixes it.
Lights: covers the cage for the night. It uncovers itself in the morning.
Scritch: head scritches. It closes its eyes and a heart pops up.
— 4 —
CARE GUIDE · おせわ

Keeping it happy おせわ

What it's telling you

! in the corner: it needs something. Check the menu or its stats.
Rain cloud: hungry or bored. Seed, millet or a game.
Blinking cross: it's ill. Time for the vet.
Droppings: give it a bath before it gets sick.
Zzz: sleeping. After 22:00, cover the cage with Light.
Wi-Fi sign: no known network, so it's resting.

Watch the crest: flat means content or sleepy, half-up curious, straight up excited. Just like a real cockatiel.

Growing up

Egghatches in minutes
Chickday 0–1
Juvenileday 1–3
Adultday 3 onward

How you care for it decides its adult feathers: lots of love gives a pied beauty, good care a pearl, so-so care a classic grey, and neglect a scruffy one. Ignore it for three whole days and it flies off to find a friend. Tap Hatch a new egg on the phone to start again.

— 5 —
PLAY & PHONE · あそぶ

Games and the phone page あそぶ

Whistle duet

Tap Whistle duet on the phone. The bird whistles a pattern on the egg: · short and — long notes. Copy it with the two big buttons on your phone. Three rounds; win two.

Peekaboo

Tap Peekaboo. It hides behind the tree on the egg (watch the tail!). Tap Peek! on your phone the moment it peeks out. Five rounds, and it gets quicker.

More on the phone

Besides care and games, the page has three more tabs:

TUNETap up to 8 notes and Teach. Then Practise together once a day, three days in a row: from then on it whistles your song by itself.
PHOTOSaves a picture of its screen.
SETTINGSWi-Fi networks, rename, updates, new egg. Tap Show code on the egg, then type the 4 digits from its screen.

When a new phone or laptop joins your Wi-Fi, it notices: crest up and a curious “?”.

— 6 —
HELP · こまったときは

If something's odd こまったときは

?Stuck on the Wi-Fi sign. It can't find a saved network. Wait a minute for JOIN OKAME-… and add your Wi-Fi again. iPhone hotspot? Turn on Maximise Compatibility and keep the hotspot screen open.
?Can't open name.local. Make sure your phone is on the same Wi-Fi. The name is shown at the bottom of the page.
?Settings code expired? Tap Show code on the egg again. Each code works for 2 minutes.
?“UPDATE” with a bar. New feathers! The bird is updating itself. Leave it plugged in for a minute.
?It flew away. Tap Hatch a new egg on the phone page.

Updates

Okame checks for new versions once a day, while it's idle. Updates are signed, so it only installs genuine ones, and if one ever misbehaves it goes back to the previous version by itself.

Good to know

Keep it dry and out of hot sun. The egg is 3D-printed PLA. Its brain is a tiny ESP32-S3 computer; its screen is 160 × 80 pixels, drawn as 40 × 20 big dots. It has no buttons on purpose: everything happens on your phone.
